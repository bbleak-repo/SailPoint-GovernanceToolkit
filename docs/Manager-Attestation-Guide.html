1. Why We Certify Privileged Access Daily

Traditional access reviews run quarterly or annually. A user is granted Domain Admin on January 3; the next review is April 1 -- for 88 days that privilege sits unexamined. If the account is compromised, the blast radius includes every system the Domain Admin can reach.

Daily delta certification changes the equation. Instead of reviewing all access once a quarter, we review only access that changed in the last 24 hours, every day.

ApproachScope per ReviewReviewer FatigueTime-to-Review
Quarterly fullHundreds of itemsHigh (rubber-stamping risk)Up to 90 days
Daily delta1-5 items (typical)Low (focused decisions)< 24 hours
Tip
Daily reviews are smaller than quarterly reviews, not more work. You are reviewing fewer items more frequently -- which leads to better decisions and stronger audit evidence.

What Triggers a Daily Campaign

A campaign is created for you when one of your direct reports receives a new privileged entitlement -- typically an Active Directory security group membership that grants elevated access (Domain Admins, Server Operators, privileged application roles, etc.). The system detects the GRANT_ACCESS event from the previous 24 hours and groups affected identities by manager.

You will not receive a campaign if nothing changed. No changes = no campaign = no action required.

2. The Shift: Application Owners to Managers

Historically, many organizations ran Source Owner campaigns -- the application owner (the person responsible for the system) reviewed who had access. This model has a fundamental limitation: the application owner knows the system but often does not know the person.

Source Owner Reviews

"Is this account configured correctly in my system?"

Cannot answer whether the person still needs access for their current role.

Manager Reviews

"Should this person have this access at all?"

You know the person, their current projects, recent role changes, and whether the access is still needed.

Only you, as the employee's manager, can answer these questions:

  • What project is your team member working on right now?
  • Have they recently changed roles, transferred teams, or shifted responsibilities?
  • Was this access a temporary need that has since expired?
  • Could another team member fulfill the same function with lower-privilege access?
Note
In SailPoint ISC, a Manager campaign automatically distributes certifications to each identity's manager. You only see your direct reports -- never someone else's team. If an identity has no manager assigned, the campaign falls back to the campaign owner or an administrator.

When you approve a privileged entitlement, you are personally attesting:

  1. This person needs this level of access right now
  2. No lower-privilege alternative exists
  3. The access aligns with their current job responsibilities
  4. You accept accountability for this decision
Warning
Auditors review certification decisions. They check who approved, when, and whether the approval pattern suggests genuine review versus rubber-stamping. Approving everything in under 10 seconds flags your decisions for additional scrutiny.

4. Communicating with Your Team about Least Privilege

As a manager, you are the bridge between security policy and your team's daily work. Here is how to have productive conversations about access rights.

4.1 The Conversation Framework

When an employee requests elevated access -- or when you see a new grant appear in a certification campaign -- ask these four questions:

The Four Questions
  1. "What specifically do you need to accomplish?"
    Understand the task, not just the access. Often the real need is narrower than the access requested.
  2. "Is there a lower-privilege way to do this?"
    Can a read-only role work instead of read-write? Can a delegated admin role replace full admin?
  3. "How long will you need this access?"
    If the answer is "for this project" or "until next Tuesday," the access should be time-bound.
  4. "What happens if you lose this access tomorrow?"
    If the answer is "nothing, I haven't used it in months," that is your signal to revoke.

4.2 Setting the Tone

Frame least privilege as professional hygiene, not punishment:

Instead of...Try...
"I'm revoking your access" "Let's right-size your access to match your current role"
"Security says you can't have this" "We're reducing the team's attack surface -- here's what that means for you"
"You don't need Domain Admin" "What specific tasks require Domain Admin? Let's find the right level of access"
"This is a compliance requirement" "This protects both you and the company -- if this account were compromised, you'd be in the blast radius"

4.3 What to Tell Your Team about Daily Campaigns

"We've moved to daily access reviews for privileged roles. This means when you get new elevated access, I'll review it within 24 hours. This is actually better for everyone -- instead of a massive quarterly review where I'm looking at hundreds of items, I'm reviewing 1-2 items with full context of what you're working on right now. If I approve it, the audit trail shows a thoughtful, timely decision. If it was a mistake or you no longer need it, we catch it immediately instead of letting it sit for months."

4.4 Handling Pushback

"I might need it later"

Keeping access "just in case" is the definition of over-provisioning. Access can be re-granted when a specific need arises -- typically within hours. The risk of an unused privileged account sitting idle for months far outweighs the inconvenience of re-requesting.

"Everyone on my old team had this"

Role transfers are the #1 source of privilege accumulation. Access appropriate for your previous role is not necessarily appropriate for your current one. This is normal -- it means the system is working correctly.

"I need it for emergencies"

Emergency access should go through a break-glass process with time-limited grants and enhanced logging -- not through a standing privileged account.

"It's too much hassle to request access every time"

If a team member needs recurring access, the right solution is a properly scoped role or access profile -- not a permanent grant of a broader privilege. Work with the IAM team to create the right entitlement.

5. The SailPoint ISC Campaign Experience

When a certification campaign is created for you, SailPoint ISC notifies you via email and an in-app notification. Here is what to expect.

5.1 Accessing Your Campaigns

  1. Log in to the SailPoint ISC admin console
  2. Navigate to Certifications from the main menu
  3. Select the Active tab to see campaigns awaiting your review
  4. Campaigns appear as cards (default) or in a table view

Each campaign card shows:

  • Campaign name (e.g., "Daily AD Delta Cert -- 2026-06-05")
  • Number of identities and access items to review
  • Deadline date
  • Progress indicator

5.2 Two Ways to Review: Identities vs Access Items

Once inside a campaign, you can toggle between two views:

Identities View (recommended for managers)

See a list of your direct reports. Click a person to see all of their access items.

Best for: "Let me review everything about Jane first, then move to John." You think about people, not abstract entitlements.

Access Items View

See a list of roles, access profiles, or entitlements. Click one to see which direct reports have it.

Best for: "Does it make sense that 5 people on my team all have Domain Admin?" Compare across your team.

5.3 What You See for Each Access Item

InformationWhat It Tells You
Access flagsIcons indicating: new access, birthright (auto-assigned), privileged, time-bound, critical data
How it was grantedRequested (you can revoke) vs. auto-assigned via role membership (acknowledge only)
Last account activityWhen the user last actively used this access (if SaaS Management data is available)
Peer comparisonWhether similar users in the same role have this access (recommendation engine)
Source activityAccount activity in the past 90 days
Tip
Pay special attention to the privileged flag and the last activity timestamp. An account flagged as privileged that has not been used in 60+ days is a strong candidate for revocation.

6. Making Decisions: Approve, Revoke, Reassign

6.1 Approving Access

Click the Approve (checkmark) icon next to an access item.

When to approve:

  • The person actively uses this access for their current job responsibilities
  • No lower-privilege alternative exists
  • The access is appropriate for their role level

What happens: Nothing changes operationally. The access remains in place, and the audit trail records your approval with a timestamp.

Important
For roles that were automatically assigned through membership criteria (birthright/lifecycle rules), the button says Acknowledge instead of Approve. You cannot revoke these -- they are controlled by the identity model. If you believe the assignment is wrong, contact the IAM team to update the membership criteria.

6.2 Revoking Access

Click the Revoke (X) icon next to an access item.

When to revoke:

  • The person no longer needs this access for their current role
  • A lower-privilege alternative exists and should be used instead
  • The access was granted temporarily and the need has passed
  • The person has changed roles, teams, or responsibilities
  • The access has not been used in an extended period

What happens after revocation:

Source TypeRemediation
Automated sources (Active Directory via SailPoint VA)ISC removes the entitlement automatically. The group membership is deleted in the target system.
Manual/disconnected sources (flat-file apps)A remediation task is created for the source owner, who must manually remove access and confirm completion.

You can optionally set a future revocation date for roles and access profiles (not individual entitlements). Useful for planned transitions.

Warning
Revocation is generally permanent. Reinstating revoked access requires a new access request through the normal process. Make sure the person truly no longer needs the access before revoking.

6.3 Reassigning a Certification

If you are not the right person to make a decision, you can reassign.

When to reassign:

  • You have a new direct report and their previous manager knows their access needs better
  • The access involves a system you are not familiar with
  • You are going on leave and cannot complete the review by the deadline

How to reassign:

  1. Navigate to the campaign
  2. Select Reassign for the certification
  3. Choose the new reviewer from the directory
  4. Provide a reason (include your name so the new reviewer can contact you)
  5. Submit

Reassignment rules:

  • You cannot reassign to the person whose access is being reviewed (ISC prevents self-certification)
  • You cannot reassign after you have already signed off
  • The campaign must still be active (not completed or past deadline)

6.4 Bulk Decisions

  1. Select items using checkboxes (header checkbox selects all on current page or entire review)
  2. Click Bulk Decisions
  3. Choose Approve or Revoke
Caution
Use bulk approve with extreme caution for privileged access. Bulk approval is the primary indicator of rubber-stamping. If auditors see that you bulk-approved 15 privileged entitlements in 3 seconds, your decisions will be flagged. Review each privileged item individually.

6.5 Adding Comments

Comments are required for role composition revocations and strongly recommended for any privileged access decision. Good examples:

  • "Employee transferred to Marketing 2026-06-01; no longer needs AD Server Operators"
  • "Temporary access for Q2 infra migration; project completed 2026-05-28"
  • "Discussed with employee -- confirms this access is no longer needed"

6.6 Revisiting Decisions

Before sign-off, click Revisit Decision to change an approval to a revocation (or vice versa). Once you sign off, your decisions are final.

7. Signing Off and Completing Your Review

When you have made decisions on all items in your certification:

1Review All Items
2Make Decisions
3Sign-Off Page
4Click Finish
5Read-Only

After sign-off:

  • Your certification moves to the Completed tab
  • Your decisions become read-only -- no further changes possible
  • Approved items remain unchanged
  • Revoked items enter the remediation pipeline
  • The audit trail permanently records your decisions, timestamps, and comments

What Happens If You Miss the Deadline

If the deadline passes before you complete your review:

  • An administrator must decide what happens to undecided items
  • Options: maintain access (items keep access) or revoke access (items removed)
  • SailPoint strongly recommends maintaining access for undecided items
  • Your incomplete review is visible to auditors and administrators
Important
Missing a deadline creates audit exposure. Regulatory auditors specifically look for incomplete reviews as evidence of control weakness. Complete your reviews before the deadline.

8. Real-World Scenarios

Scenario 1: New Hire with Inherited Access

You receive a campaign card showing your new hire, Alex, with 8 entitlements including Domain Admin and Exchange Admin.

What happened:

Alex was onboarded from a template that included access from a previous role holder. The template was too broad.

What to do:
  1. Switch to Identities view, click on Alex
  2. Review each entitlement individually
  3. Revoke Domain Admin and Exchange Admin -- Alex is a junior developer
  4. Approve development environment access that matches Alex's actual role
  5. Add a comment: "New hire; right-sizing access to junior developer role"
  6. Contact the IAM team about updating the onboarding template
Scenario 2: Role Transfer with Access Accumulation

Your team member Jamie transferred from Infrastructure to Application Development 3 months ago. A daily campaign shows a GRANT_ACCESS for a new developer tool, but you notice Jamie still has 4 infrastructure entitlements from the old role.

What happened:

The new grant triggered the campaign, surfacing existing access. Infrastructure access was never cleaned up during the transfer.

What to do:
  1. Approve the new developer tool entitlement (appropriate for current role)
  2. Revoke the 4 infrastructure entitlements (no longer appropriate)
  3. Add comments explaining the role transfer context
  4. Have a quick conversation with Jamie about the cleanup
Scenario 3: The "I Need It for Emergencies" Request

Chris, a senior engineer, has Domain Admin. When you ask, Chris says: "I only use it during outages -- maybe once a quarter."

What to do:
  1. Revoke the standing Domain Admin access
  2. Work with the IAM team to set up a just-in-time (JIT) access or break-glass procedure
  3. Explain: "We're replacing standing admin access with an emergency access process. When you need it, you'll get it quickly -- but it won't sit unused between incidents."
Scenario 4: Legitimate Privileged Access

Database administrator Pat receives a campaign showing DB Admin access across three production database servers.

What to do:
  1. Confirm Pat is still actively working as a DBA (yes)
  2. Confirm the scope matches current assignment (these three servers, not others)
  3. Approve all three with a comment: "Active DBA; primary administrator per current assignment"

Not every review results in a revocation. The goal is informed decisions, not maximum revocations.

Scenario 5: Access You Cannot Evaluate

You receive a certification for "SCCM_PatchAdmin_Tier2." You have no idea whether your team member needs this.

What to do:
  1. Reassign to the application owner or technical lead who manages SCCM
  2. Include a note: "Reassigning -- I am not the appropriate reviewer for SCCM entitlements."

Reassigning is always better than guessing.

9. Avoiding Rubber-Stamping

Rubber-stamping -- approving everything without genuine review -- is the single biggest risk to certification program effectiveness.

How Rubber-Stamping Is Detected

PatternWhat It Suggests
All items approved in < 10 secondsNo individual review occurred
100% approval rate over multiple campaignsBlanket approval without evaluation
No comments on any decisionNo documented reasoning
Bulk approve on privileged itemsHigh-risk decisions made without examination
Campaign completed within seconds of openingItems not even read

How to Avoid It

  • Review each privileged item individually -- take at least 15-30 seconds per item
  • Add a brief comment for non-obvious approvals: "Active project lead; requires prod DB write through Q3"
  • Revoke items you know are wrong -- a 100% approval rate draws auditor attention
  • Ask your team if unsure -- a quick message is faster than explaining a rubber-stamping finding to an auditor
Critical
Your certification decisions are your personal attestation that the access is appropriate. In a regulatory audit, you may be interviewed about specific approvals. "I just approved everything" is not an acceptable response.

10. The Least-Privilege Mindset

Least privilege is not about making people's jobs harder -- it is about ensuring that everyone has exactly the access they need and nothing more.

For Your Team Members

  • Reduced personal risk: A compromised minimal-privilege account causes contained damage. A compromised Domain Admin account triggers a major security incident.
  • Cleaner access profile: No confusion about what someone should or should not be doing.
  • Faster incident response: Investigators check privileged accounts first. Fewer = fewer people in forensic interviews.

For You as a Manager

  • Accountability clarity: Fewer unnecessary privileges means less exposure.
  • Audit readiness: A documented, recent certification decision with a clear comment is your best defense.
  • Team protection: If a team member makes an accidental change with privileges they should not have had, the investigation starts with "who approved this access?"

For the Organization

  • Attack surface reduction: Every unnecessary privilege is a potential entry point.
  • Regulatory compliance: Compliance and audit requirements all mandate documented access review evidence.
  • Insurance and liability: Cyber insurance providers increasingly examine privilege management practices.

11. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How often will I receive certification campaigns?
Only when one of your direct reports receives a new privileged entitlement. If your team's access is stable, you may go days or weeks without a campaign.
Q: How long do I have to complete a review?
Check the deadline shown on the campaign card. Daily delta campaigns typically have a 24-72 hour window. Complete your review the same day if possible.
Q: What if I approve something and later realize it should be revoked?
Before sign-off, use Revisit Decision to change your decision. After sign-off, contact the IAM team for a separate revocation request.
Q: What if my direct report says they need access I revoked?
They can submit a new access request through the standard process. This creates a documented request/approval chain rather than relying on inherited or stale access.
Q: I received a campaign for someone who no longer reports to me.
Reassign the certification to the person's current manager. This happens when the HR system has not yet updated the manager-identity relationship.
Q: Can I delegate my reviews to someone on my team?
You can reassign individual certifications, but you remain accountable as the manager. If you regularly delegate, work with the IAM team to update routing.
Q: Why can I only acknowledge some items, not approve or revoke them?
Items granted through automated membership criteria (birthright roles, lifecycle state assignments) cannot be revoked through certification. Contact the IAM team to review the criteria if the assignment seems wrong.
Q: What is the difference between a role, an access profile, and an entitlement?
Entitlement: A single permission in a target system (e.g., AD group "Domain Admins").
Access Profile: A bundle of entitlements from one source (e.g., "AD Standard User" = 3 AD groups).
Role: A business concept bundling access profiles across multiple sources (e.g., "IT Administrator" = AD Admin + ServiceNow Admin + SCCM Admin).
Q: What does the "privileged" flag mean?
The IAM team has classified certain entitlements as privileged based on their risk level (domain admin, server operator, DBA access, etc.). Items with this flag deserve your closest attention.

12. Quick-Reference Decision Checklist

Before approving or revoking a privileged access item, run through this checklist:

  • Current role match: Does this access align with the person's current job responsibilities (not a previous role)?
  • Active use: Has the person used this access recently, or is it sitting idle?
  • Scope appropriateness: Is this the minimum level of access needed, or does a lower-privilege alternative exist?
  • Time-bound need: Is this permanent access, or should it expire on a specific date?
  • Segregation of duties: Does approving this create a conflict with other access (e.g., both "create purchase orders" and "approve purchase orders")?
  • Peer comparison: Do similar employees in the same role have this access, or is this person an outlier?
Tip
You do not need to check every box for every item. For standard access, a quick role-match check may suffice. For privileged access, work through the full list.
