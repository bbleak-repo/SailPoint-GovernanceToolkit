Quick Start: Complete Your Review in 5 Minutes

You got an email saying you have access to review. Here is exactly what to do.

You cannot break anything
While you are reviewing, nothing changes. Access is only affected after you click Sign Off at the end. Until then, you can change any decision.
  1. Click the link in your email. Or go to [YOUR-ORG.identitynow.com] and log in with your corporate credentials.
  2. You will see your team members who received new access. Click on a person to see the details.
  3. For each item: Approve (checkmark) or Revoke (X). If you do not recognize the system, click Reassign to send it to someone who can evaluate it.
  4. Add a brief comment for important decisions. One sentence is enough.
  5. Click Sign Off when done. Review the summary, then click Finish. You are done.

2. Making Decisions: Approve, Revoke, Reassign

Approve

Click the checkmark. Means "yes, this person should have this." Access stays in place.

Note
Some items show Acknowledge instead of Approve. These are automatically assigned based on job title/department and cannot be changed through a review.

Revoke

Click the X. Means "no, remove this." Connected systems remove access automatically; others create a manual task.

Mistakes are fixable
Before sign-off: Revisit Decision to change it. After sign-off: contact the IAM team at [IAM-TEAM-EMAIL]. Access can be re-requested (typically takes [X hours/days]).

Reassign

Send the review to someone better positioned. Use when you do not recognize the system, the person recently transferred to you, or you are going on leave.

Tip
Reassigning is the responsible thing to do when you lack context. Always better than guessing.

Bulk Decisions

Select multiple items and click Bulk Decisions. Use carefully for elevated access -- review those individually.

Checklist for Elevated Access

  • Current role match: Does this fit their current job?
  • Active use: Have they used it recently?
  • Minimum needed: Is there a narrower option?
  • Time-bound? Should it expire on a date?
  • Conflict check: Does this create a conflict with other access?
  • Peer comparison: Do others in the same role have this?

After Finish: decisions are permanent, approved items stay, revoked items begin removal.

If you miss the deadline: An administrator handles undecided items (typically by keeping access). Incomplete reviews are visible to compliance teams. Complete reviews the same day -- 5-10 minutes.

4. Common Scenarios

New hire with too much access Mixed

Alex has admin permissions from a broad onboarding template. Revoke the admin access, Approve what matches the actual role.

Team member transferred with old access Mixed

Jamie's old infrastructure permissions were never cleaned up. Approve the new developer access, Revoke the 4 old infrastructure items.

"I only use it during emergencies" Revoke

Chris has standing admin access used once a quarter. Revoke and set up on-demand emergency access with the IAM team.

Access that is clearly appropriate Approve

DBA Pat actively manages these three servers daily. Approve with a comment. The goal is informed decisions, not maximum revocations.

Access you do not recognize Reassign

"SCCM_PatchAdmin_Tier2" means nothing to you. Reassign to the system owner. Always better than guessing.

Your team only uses CRM, but you see unfamiliar items Mixed

Approve what you recognize (CRM, phone). For unknown items, ask the team member or Reassign.

You are a new manager and inherited a team Reassign

Make decisions where you can. Reassign unfamiliar items to the previous manager or team lead. Contact the IAM team to understand your team's typical access.

5. Why We Review Access Daily

Quarterly reviews mean access goes unexamined for up to 90 days. Daily delta reviews cover only new or changed access -- typically 1-5 items. Smaller scope, lower fatigue, faster response.

6. Why Managers Review (Not System Admins)

System admins know the system but not the people. Only you know whether a team member still needs access for their current role.

System Admin

"Is the account configured right?"

Manager

"Should this person have this access?"

7. Talking to Your Team

Four Questions to Ask

  1. "What specifically do you need to accomplish?"
  2. "Is there a way to do this with less access?"
  3. "How long will you need this?"
  4. "What happens if you lose this access tomorrow?"

Setting the Tone

Instead of...Try...
"I'm revoking your access""Let's right-size your access to your current role"
"Security says you can't""We're reducing the team's risk exposure"
"You don't need admin""What tasks need admin? Let's find the right level"

8. What Good Reviews Look Like

The goal is informed, documented decisions -- not maximum revocations.

Good PatternWhat It Shows
Each item reviewed individuallyYou considered each decision
Brief comments on key decisionsReasoning documented for compliance
Mix of approvals and revocationsGenuine evaluation
Completed before deadlineTimely and responsible
Bottom line
Take a moment to look at each item. Add a brief comment for anything non-obvious. That is all it takes. Your decisions are part of the compliance record and protect both you and the company.

9. Frequently Asked Questions

How often will I receive reviews?
Only when a direct report gets new elevated access. No changes = no campaign.
How long do I have?
Check the campaign card. Typically 24-72 hours. Try to complete same-day.
What if I make a mistake?
Before sign-off: Revisit Decision. After: contact [IAM-TEAM-EMAIL]. Mistakes are fixable.
My team member needs access I revoked.
They submit a new access request. For urgent needs, the IAM team can expedite.
I got a review for someone who no longer reports to me.
Reassign to their current manager.
Why can I only Acknowledge some items?
Automatically assigned access (based on job title/department) cannot be changed through reviews. Contact the IAM team if the assignment seems wrong.
I see system names I don't recognize.
Reassign to the system owner, or ask the team member if they use it.
What does "privileged" mean?
The IT team flagged this as elevated risk (server admin, database admin, etc.). Give these items extra attention.
What is the difference between a role, access profile, and entitlement?
Entitlement: one permission. Access Profile: a bundle from one system. Role: a business-level bundle across systems. Think: ingredients, recipes, complete meals.

10. Glossary

TermPlain-English Definition
Access ProfileA bundle of permissions from one system (e.g., "Standard CRM Access" = 3 permissions)
Birthright AccessAccess automatically given based on job title/department. Cannot be changed through a review.
CampaignA review cycle with a deadline. You receive one when your team's access needs checking.
Daily Delta ReviewA review of only access that changed in the last 24 hours. "Delta" means "change."
EntitlementA single permission (e.g., membership in a group called "CRM_Users")
IAM TeamThe Identity and Access Management team. Contact: [IAM-TEAM-EMAIL]
IdentityA person as represented in the system. SailPoint's word for "employee" or "user."
Least PrivilegeOnly the access needed for your current job -- like only having keys to rooms you use.
Privileged AccessElevated permissions (server admin, database admin, etc.). Higher risk = more scrutiny.
Privilege CreepGradual buildup of access over time, especially during role changes. Daily reviews prevent this.
RoleA business-level bundle spanning multiple systems (e.g., "Customer Service Rep" = CRM + phone + KB)
SOX / SOC 2Regulatory standards requiring documented access reviews. Your reviews are part of this evidence.
SourceA connected system (Active Directory, your CRM, HR system) from which permissions are imported.
