1. Why We Certify Privileged Access Daily

Traditional access reviews run quarterly or annually. A user is granted Domain Admin on January 3; the next review is April 1 -- for 88 days that privilege sits unexamined. Daily delta certification changes the equation.

ApproachScope per ReviewReviewer FatigueTime-to-Review
Quarterly fullHundreds of itemsHigh (rubber-stamping risk)Up to 90 days
Daily delta1-5 items (typical)Low (focused decisions)< 24 hours
Tip
Daily reviews are smaller than quarterly reviews, not more work. You are reviewing fewer items more frequently -- leading to better decisions and stronger audit evidence.

What Triggers a Daily Campaign

A campaign is created for you when one of your direct reports receives a new privileged entitlement -- typically an AD security group membership granting elevated access. The system detects the GRANT_ACCESS event from the previous 24 hours and groups affected identities by manager.

You will not receive a campaign if nothing changed. No changes = no campaign = no action required.

2. The Shift: Application Owners to Managers

The application owner knows the system but often does not know the person. Only you, as the employee's manager, can answer the critical question: "Should this person have this access at all?"

Source Owner Reviews

"Is this account configured correctly in my system?"

Cannot answer whether the person still needs access for their current role.

Manager Reviews

"Should this person have this access at all?"

You know the person, their projects, recent role changes, and whether access is still needed.

Only you can answer:

  • What project is your team member working on right now?
  • Have they recently changed roles, transferred teams, or shifted responsibilities?
  • Was this access a temporary need that has since expired?
  • Could another team member fulfill the same function with lower-privilege access?
Note
In SailPoint ISC, a Manager campaign automatically distributes certifications to each identity's manager. You only see your direct reports -- never someone else's team.

When you approve a privileged entitlement, you are personally attesting:

  1. This person needs this level of access right now
  2. No lower-privilege alternative exists

Warning

4. Communicating with Your Team about Least Privilege

You are the bridge between security policy and your team's daily work. Here is how to have productive conversations about access rights.

4.1 The Conversation Framework

The Four Questions
  1. "What specifically do you need to accomplish?"
    Understand the task, not just the access. Often the real need is narrower.
  2. "Is there a lower-privilege way to do this?"
    Read-only instead of read-write? Delegated admin instead of full admin?
  3. "How long will you need this access?"
    "For this project" = time-bound. Permanent grants for temporary needs cause privilege creep.
  4. "What happens if you lose this access tomorrow?"
    "Nothing, I haven't used it in months" = your signal to revoke.

4.2 Setting the Tone

Frame least privilege as professional hygiene, not punishment:

Instead of...Try...
"I'm revoking your access""Let's right-size your access to match your current role"
"Security says you can't have this""We're reducing the team's attack surface -- here's what that means for you"
"You don't need Domain Admin""What specific tasks require Domain Admin? Let's find the right level"
"This is a compliance requirement""This protects both you and the company -- if compromised, you'd be in the blast radius"

4.3 What to Tell Your Team about Daily Campaigns

"We've moved to daily access reviews for privileged roles. This means when you get new elevated access, I'll review it within 24 hours. This is actually better for everyone -- instead of a massive quarterly review where I'm looking at hundreds of items, I'm reviewing 1-2 items with full context of what you're working on right now."

4.4 Handling Pushback

"I might need it later"

Keeping access "just in case" is the definition of over-provisioning. Access can be re-granted when a specific need arises -- typically within hours.

"Everyone on my old team had this"

Role transfers are the #1 source of privilege accumulation. Access appropriate for your previous role is not necessarily appropriate for your current one.

"I need it for emergencies"

Emergency access should go through a break-glass process with time-limited grants and enhanced logging -- not through a standing privileged account.

"It's too much hassle to request access every time"

The right solution is a properly scoped role or access profile -- not a permanent grant of a broader privilege. Work with the IAM team.

5. The SailPoint ISC Campaign Experience

When a certification campaign is created for you, SailPoint ISC notifies you via email and in-app notification. Here is what to expect.

5.1 Accessing Your Campaigns

  1. Log in to the SailPoint ISC admin console
  2. Navigate to Certifications from the main menu
  3. Select the Active tab to see campaigns awaiting your review
  4. Campaigns appear as cards (default) or in table view

Each campaign card shows: campaign name, number of identities/access items, deadline date, and progress indicator.

5.2 Two Ways to Review: Identities vs Access Items

Identities View (recommended)

See a list of your direct reports. Click a person to see all their access items.

Best for: "Let me review everything about Jane, then move to John."

Access Items View

See roles, access profiles, or entitlements. Click one to see which direct reports have it.

Best for: "Does it make sense that 5 people have Domain Admin?"

5.3 What You See for Each Access Item

InformationWhat It Tells You

How it was grantedRequested (revocable) vs. auto-assigned (acknowledge only)

Tip
Pay special attention to the privileged flag and last activity timestamp. Privileged access not used in 60+ days is a strong candidate for revocation.

6. Making Decisions: Approve, Revoke, Reassign

The three actions available to you and when to use each one.

6.1 Approving Access

Click the Approve (checkmark) icon. Use when the person actively uses the access for current responsibilities and no lower-privilege alternative exists.

What happens: Access remains in place. The audit trail records your approval with timestamp.

Important
For roles automatically assigned through membership criteria, the button says Acknowledge. You cannot revoke these -- contact the IAM team to update criteria.

6.2 Revoking Access

Click the Revoke (X) icon. Use when access is no longer needed, was temporary, or a lower-privilege alternative should be used instead.

Source TypeRemediation
Automated sources (AD via SailPoint VA)ISC removes the entitlement automatically
Manual/disconnected sourcesRemediation task created for the source owner
Warning
Revocation is generally permanent. Reinstating access requires a new request. Confirm the person truly no longer needs the access.

6.3 Reassigning a Certification

Use when you are not the right person to decide -- new direct report, unfamiliar system, or you're going on leave.

  1. Select Reassign for the certification
  2. Choose the new reviewer from the directory
  3. Provide a reason (include your name for follow-up)
  4. Submit

Rules: Cannot reassign to the person being reviewed (no self-certification). Cannot reassign after sign-off. Campaign must be active.

6.4 Bulk Decisions

Select items with checkboxes, then click Bulk Decisions to approve or revoke.

Caution
Bulk approve is the primary indicator of rubber-stamping. Auditors flag bulk-approved privileged entitlements. Review each privileged item individually.

6.5 Adding Comments

Required for role composition revocations. Strongly recommended for all privileged access decisions. Examples:

  • "Employee transferred to Marketing 2026-06-01; no longer needs AD Server Operators"
  • "Temporary access for Q2 infra migration; project completed 2026-05-28"
  • "Discussed with employee -- confirms access is no longer needed"

6.6 Revisiting Decisions

Before sign-off, click Revisit Decision to change an approval to a revocation or vice versa. After sign-off, decisions are final.

7. Signing Off and Completing Your Review

The final step: reviewing your summary and making it permanent.

After sign-off:

  • Your certification moves to the Completed tab
  • Your decisions become read-only
  • Approved items remain unchanged
  • Revoked items enter the remediation pipeline
  • Audit trail permanently records decisions, timestamps, and comments

What Happens If You Miss the Deadline

  • An administrator decides what happens to undecided items
  • Options: maintain access or revoke access
  • SailPoint recommends maintaining access for undecided items
  • Your incomplete review is visible to auditors
Important
Missing a deadline creates audit exposure. Auditors look for incomplete reviews as evidence of control weakness. Complete your reviews before the deadline.

8. Real-World Scenarios

Five common situations and exactly how to handle them.

Scenario 1: New Hire with Inherited Access

Your new hire Alex has 8 entitlements including Domain Admin and Exchange Admin -- onboarded from a template that was too broad.

What to do:
  1. Switch to Identities view, click Alex
  2. Review each entitlement individually
  3. Revoke Domain Admin and Exchange Admin (junior developer)
  4. Approve development environment access matching actual role
  5. Comment: "New hire; right-sizing access to junior developer role"
  6. Contact IAM about updating the onboarding template
Scenario 2: Role Transfer with Access Accumulation

Jamie transferred from Infrastructure to your team 3 months ago. A new GRANT_ACCESS for a developer tool surfaces 4 old infrastructure entitlements.

What to do:
  1. Approve the new developer tool (appropriate for current role)
  2. Revoke the 4 infrastructure entitlements (no longer appropriate)
  3. Add comments explaining the role transfer
  4. Quick conversation with Jamie: "I cleaned up your old infra access -- let me know if you hit any blockers."
Scenario 3: The "I Need It for Emergencies" Request

Chris, a senior engineer, has Domain Admin. "I only use it during outages -- maybe once a quarter."

What to do:
  1. Revoke the standing Domain Admin access
  2. Work with IAM to set up just-in-time (JIT) or break-glass access
  3. Explain: "We're replacing standing admin with an emergency access process. You'll get it quickly when needed, but it won't sit unused between incidents."
Scenario 4: Legitimate Privileged Access

DBA Pat has DB Admin on three production database servers.

What to do:
  1. Confirm Pat is still actively working as a DBA (yes)
  2. Confirm scope matches current assignment (these three servers)
  3. Approve all three: "Active DBA; primary administrator per current assignment"

The goal is informed decisions, not maximum revocations.

Scenario 5: Access You Cannot Evaluate

You receive a certification for "SCCM_PatchAdmin_Tier2" -- a system you've never heard of.

What to do:
  1. Reassign to the application owner or technical lead for SCCM
  2. Note: "Reassigning -- I am not the appropriate reviewer for SCCM entitlements."

Reassigning is always better than guessing.

9. Avoiding Rubber-Stamping

Approving everything without genuine review is the single biggest risk to certification program effectiveness.

How Rubber-Stamping Is Detected

PatternWhat It Suggests
All items approved in < 10 secondsNo individual review occurred
100% approval rate over multiple campaignsBlanket approval without evaluation
No comments on any decisionNo documented reasoning
Bulk approve on privileged itemsHigh-risk decisions without examination
Campaign completed within seconds of openingItems not even read

How to Avoid It

  • Review each privileged item individually -- take at least 15-30 seconds per item
  • Add a brief comment for non-obvious approvals
  • Revoke items you know are wrong -- 100% approval draws auditor attention
  • Ask your team if unsure -- a quick message beats explaining a finding to an auditor
Critical
Your certification decisions are your personal attestation that the access is appropriate. In a SOX or regulatory audit, you may be interviewed about specific approvals. "I just approved everything" is not an acceptable response.

10. The Least-Privilege Mindset

Least privilege ensures that everyone has exactly the access they need and nothing more. Here is why this matters at every level.

For Your Team Members

  • Reduced personal risk: Compromised minimal-privilege account = contained damage. Compromised Domain Admin = major security incident.
  • Cleaner access profile: No confusion about what someone should or should not be doing.
  • Faster incident response: Investigators check privileged accounts first. Fewer = fewer people in forensic interviews.

For You as a Manager

  • Accountability clarity: Fewer unnecessary privileges = less exposure.
  • Audit readiness: A documented, recent certification decision with a clear comment is your best defense.
  • Team protection: If a team member makes an accidental change with unneeded privileges, the investigation starts with "who approved this access?"

For the Organization

  • Attack surface reduction: Every unnecessary privilege is a potential entry point.
  • Regulatory compliance: SOX 404, SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, NIST 800-53 all require documented access review evidence.
  • Insurance and liability: Cyber insurance providers examine privilege management practices.

11. Frequently Asked Questions

Click any question to expand the answer.

How often will I receive certification campaigns?
Only when a direct report receives a new privileged entitlement. If your team's access is stable, you may go days or weeks without a campaign.
How long do I have to complete a review?
Check the deadline on the campaign card. Daily delta campaigns typically have a 24-72 hour window. Complete your review the same day if possible.
What if I approve something and later realize it should be revoked?
Before sign-off, use Revisit Decision. After sign-off, contact the IAM team for a separate revocation request.
What if my direct report says they need access I revoked?
They submit a new access request through the standard process. This creates a documented request/approval chain rather than relying on stale access.
I received a campaign for someone who no longer reports to me.
Reassign the certification to the person's current manager with a note explaining the reporting change.
Can I delegate my reviews to someone on my team?
You can reassign individual certifications, but you remain accountable. If you regularly delegate, work with IAM to update routing.
Why can I only acknowledge some items, not approve or revoke them?
Items granted through automated membership criteria (birthright roles, lifecycle states) cannot be revoked through certification. Contact IAM to review the criteria.
What is the difference between a role, an access profile, and an entitlement?
Entitlement: Single permission in a target system (e.g., AD group "Domain Admins").
Access Profile: Bundle of entitlements from one source (e.g., "AD Standard User" = 3 AD groups).
Role: Business concept bundling access profiles across sources (e.g., "IT Administrator").
What does the "privileged" flag mean?
The IAM team has classified certain entitlements as privileged based on risk level (domain admin, server operator, DBA, etc.). These deserve your closest attention.

12. Quick-Reference Decision Checklist

Before approving or revoking a privileged access item, run through this checklist.

  • Current role match: Does this access align with the person's current job responsibilities (not a previous role)?
  • Active use: Has the person used this access recently, or is it sitting idle?
  • Scope appropriateness: Is this the minimum level of access needed, or does a lower-privilege alternative exist?
  • Time-bound need: Is this permanent access, or should it expire on a specific date?
  • Segregation of duties: Does approving this create a conflict with other access (e.g., both "create purchase orders" and "approve purchase orders")?
  • Peer comparison: Do similar employees in the same role have this access, or is this person an outlier?
Tip
You do not need to check every box for every item. For standard access, a quick role-match check suffices. For privileged access, work through the full list.

13. Glossary

Key terms for managers working with access certification campaigns.

Attestation
Your formal confirmation that an access assignment is appropriate
Campaign
A review cycle containing one or more certifications assigned to reviewers
Certification
Your specific review assignment -- the identities and access items you must evaluate
Delta Certification
A campaign that only includes access changed since the last review (new grants)
Entitlement
A specific permission in a target system (e.g., AD group membership)
Identity
A person (your direct report) as represented in the governance system
Least Privilege
Only the minimum access needed for current job function
Privilege Creep
Gradual accumulation of access rights over time, especially during role transfers
Remediation
Removing access after revocation -- automatic (connected) or manual (disconnected)
Rubber-Stamping
Approving all items without genuine review -- a compliance risk and audit finding
Sign-Off
Final submission of decisions; locks the review permanently
Source
A connected system (AD, Entra ID, ServiceNow) from which accounts are aggregated
