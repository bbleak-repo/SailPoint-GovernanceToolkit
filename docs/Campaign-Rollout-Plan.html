Daily Access Certification -- Rollout Plan

Communication kit, timeline, email templates, and success metrics for launching daily privileged access reviews with people-managers.

Owner: IAM Team Audience: IAM Team, IT Leadership, Change Management Classification: Internal Version: 1.0

Executive Summary

We are transitioning from quarterly full-scope access reviews to daily delta reviews for privileged access. Instead of reviewing hundreds of items once a quarter (leading to rubber-stamping and reviewer fatigue), managers will review only newly granted privileged access items each day -- typically 1-5 items, taking 5-10 minutes.

This rollout plan covers:

  • A 4-phase rollout over 6 weeks (pilot, expand, full launch, steady state)
  • Ready-to-send email templates for every stage
  • Manager-facing documentation suite (guide, quick-reference card, decision tree)
  • Success metrics and KPIs to track program effectiveness
  • Escalation procedures and support model

Rollout Timeline

Phase 1
Pilot (Weeks 1-2)
10-15 managers in IT and Security -- teams familiar with access concepts
Week 1, Monday
Send Pre-Launch Announcement email to pilot group
Week 1, Wednesday
15-minute training session (optional, recorded) -- walk through the guide and a sample campaign
Week 1, Thursday
Activate daily campaigns for pilot group. Send First Campaign email with guide + quick-ref attached
Week 2, Monday
Check completion rates. Send Follow-Up Check-In to anyone who has not completed
Week 2, Friday
Collect pilot feedback. Adjust documentation based on pain points. Measure: completion rate, avg time, support tickets
Pilot success criteria
Before moving to Phase 2: >80% completion rate, <3 support tickets per day, no critical documentation gaps reported.
Phase 2
Expansion (Weeks 3-4)
All managers in Engineering, Operations, and Finance
Week 3, Monday
Send Pre-Launch Announcement to expansion group (updated with pilot learnings)
Week 3, Wednesday
Post guide + quick-ref + decision tree on intranet/SharePoint. Send Teams announcement.
Week 3, Thursday
Activate daily campaigns for expansion group. Pilot managers available as peer mentors.
Week 4, Monday
Check completion rates. Identify managers struggling -- offer 1-on-1 walkthroughs.
Week 4, Friday
Review metrics. Prepare for full launch.
Phase 3
Full Launch (Week 5)
All remaining managers organization-wide
Week 5, Monday
Leadership sends Executive Announcement (VP/CISO-level). IAM sends Pre-Launch to remaining managers.
Week 5, Wednesday
Activate daily campaigns for all managers. Deploy documentation to all channels.
Week 5, Friday
First completion rate check across full population. Escalate non-completions to skip-level managers.
Phase 4
Steady State (Week 6+)
Ongoing operations, continuous improvement
Week 6
Weekly metrics dashboard published to IAM + CISO. Identify repeat non-completers.
Month 2
Reduce check-in frequency to bi-weekly. Add new manager onboarding to HR process.
Quarter 2
Retrospective: review metrics, manager feedback, documentation updates. Present results to audit committee.

Success Metrics

Campaign Completion Rate
>90%
Percentage of campaigns completed before deadline. Target: >90% within 30 days of launch.
Average Review Time
<15 min
Median time from campaign creation to sign-off. Target: under 15 minutes for daily deltas.
Revocation Rate
5-25%
Percentage of items revoked. Below 5% suggests rubber-stamping; above 25% suggests over-provisioning.
Support Ticket Volume
<5/week
IAM team tickets related to certification confusion. Declining trend indicates documentation is working.
Rubber-Stamping Score
<10%
Percentage of managers who approve 100% of items in under 60 seconds. Target: under 10%.
Comment Rate on Privileged Items
>50%
Percentage of privileged access decisions with a comment. Indicates thoughtful review.
Reporting cadence
Publish these metrics weekly during Phases 1-3, bi-weekly during Phase 4, and monthly at steady state. Share with CISO and audit committee quarterly.

Change Management Considerations

Resistance Patterns and Mitigation

Expected ResistanceRoot CauseMitigation
"I don't have time for this" Perceives daily reviews as a new burden Emphasize: 5-10 min, smaller than quarterly, only when changes occur. Compare to timesheet approval.
"I don't understand what I'm looking at" Non-technical manager, unfamiliar systems Quick-ref card, decision tree, reassign option, 1-on-1 walkthrough offer, peer mentors from pilot
"This is IT's job, not mine" Does not understand the manager's unique knowledge Executive announcement framing (VP/CISO level), Section 6 of guide explains why managers are essential
"What if I make a mistake?" Fear of consequences "You cannot break anything" messaging, Revisit Decision before sign-off, IAM team fix-it support, Scenario 8
"Why is this daily? Quarterly was fine" Change fatigue, questions value Data: 74% breach stat, rubber-stamping evidence from quarterly reviews, daily = smaller not more

Success Factors

  • Executive sponsorship: CISO or VP sends the executive announcement (Template D). Without visible leadership support, managers treat it as optional.
  • Peer advocacy: Pilot managers become informal advocates. "It's easy, I do it in 5 minutes" from a peer is more convincing than any documentation.
  • Documentation accessibility: Guide posted on intranet, quick-ref printable, decision tree on desk. Multiple formats for multiple preferences.
  • Low-friction support: IAM team available via email, phone, and Teams. Offer to walk first-timers through their first campaign live.
  • Visible metrics: Share completion rates (anonymized by team, not individual) to create positive social pressure.
  • Continuous improvement: Collect feedback after Phases 1-3. Update documentation based on real manager questions. Version the guide.
  • HR integration: Add access review responsibilities to manager onboarding checklist. New managers get the guide on day 1.

Leadership Talking Points

For VPs and directors to use in team meetings or all-hands when introducing the program:

The Elevator Pitch (30 seconds)

"We're starting daily access reviews for elevated permissions. When someone on your team gets new admin access, you'll get a quick review -- 5-10 minutes. You'll confirm the access is right for their current role. This replaces the quarterly review that nobody liked, and it's a core part of our compliance program."

The Accountability Frame

"As managers, we're the ones who know our teams best. We know who's working on what project, who just transferred, who no longer needs that old access. That's why this lands with us, not the IT department. We're making sure our people have the right access -- no more, no less."

Handling the "More Work" Objection

"I hear you -- nobody wants more tasks. The daily review is actually less work than the quarterly one. Instead of reviewing hundreds of items in one sitting and rushing through them, you're reviewing 1-5 items when they're fresh. It's like reviewing timesheets daily instead of doing three months at once."

The Risk Frame (for skeptics)

"Every unnecessary privileged account is a potential entry point for an attacker. Three quarters of data breaches involve compromised privileged credentials. These reviews directly reduce our attack surface. They also protect your team members -- if an account is compromised, less access means less damage."
