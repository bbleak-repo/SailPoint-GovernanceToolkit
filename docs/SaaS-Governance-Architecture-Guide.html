SaaS Governance Architecture Guide
Practical identity governance at scale without custom connectors or unlimited budget

Governing 100+ Apps Without 100 Connectors

This guide is for IAM teams at organizations with 1,000-10,000 identities, 1-3 IAM engineers, 100+ SaaS applications, and a SailPoint ISC budget that does not include building 50 custom connectors at $15-60K each.

The answer is not "connect everything to SailPoint." The answer is a tiered governance model where your IdP does the heavy lifting and SailPoint provides the governance framework on top.

Who this is for
  • IAM architects designing a governance program for 100+ apps
  • CISOs who need to explain the strategy to auditors and the board
  • IAM engineers implementing the technical configuration
  • Anyone who has been told "just build a connector" and knows that is not realistic

The Core Insight

Most modern SaaS apps support SSO group-to-role mapping. If you design your IdP groups at the role level (not just the app level), then certifying the group membership in SailPoint IS certifying the in-app authorization -- without a single custom connector.

Surface-Level Governance

One group per app: Salesforce_Users

Certify: "Does Jane have access to Salesforce?" -- says nothing about what she can do inside it.

Role-Level Governance

Groups per role: SF_Admin, SF_Standard, SF_ReadOnly

Certify: "Does Jane have Salesforce Admin access?" -- now you are governing authorization.

The Problem: Three Colliding Constraints

Understanding why the obvious answers do not work for most organizations.

Constraint 1: Source Limits

SailPoint ISC limits the number of connected sources by tier. Every connector -- including flat file imports for disconnected apps -- consumes a source slot.

An org with 100 disconnected apps consuming flat file sources would exhaust even the Business tier. Business Plus at $9-13/identity/month for 5,000 identities is $540K-780K/year -- out of reach for many mid-market organizations.

Constraint 2: Engineering Capacity

Custom connectors cost $15K-60K each in professional services, or weeks of internal engineering time. At 50+ apps, that is $750K-3M in connector development -- and ongoing maintenance when APIs change.

Most mid-market IAM teams have 1-3 engineers. They cannot build and maintain 50 custom connectors while also running the governance program.

Constraint 3: App Team Burden

Every flat file integration requires the app team to build and maintain a daily export pipeline. For 100 app teams, that is 100 separate conversations, 100 export scripts to maintain, and 100 potential failure points.

Tier 1 Deep Connectors -- Full Entitlement Governance

What: SailPoint connects directly to the application with a native connector. Aggregates users, entitlements, roles, permissions. Full provisioning. Entitlement-level certification.

Apps: 5-10 critical systems where in-app permissions are complex and high-risk. Active Directory, Entra ID, SAP, Salesforce, ServiceNow, CyberArk, Workday.

Source slots: 5-10 (one per app)

Effort per app: 1-8 weeks config

Governance level: Full authorization governance. Certify individual permissions, roles, profiles, entitlements within the app.

When to use: The app has a complex access model (roles + permissions + profiles), the data is compliance-critical, and incorrect in-app permissions create real business risk.

Tier 2 Role-Level IdP Groups -- Authorization Governance via Proxy

What: Instead of connecting to the app, you create IdP groups that map to specific roles within the app. The app is configured to assign roles based on SSO group claims or SCIM group membership. SailPoint certifies the group membership -- which IS the role assignment.

Apps: 50-200 SaaS apps federated to Okta or Entra ID that support group-to-role mapping.

Source slots: 0 additional (uses existing Okta/Entra source)

Effort per app: 2-4 hours config

Governance level: Authorization governance at the role level. "Does Jane need Salesforce Admin access?" not just "Does Jane need Salesforce?"

When to use: The app supports SSO group-to-role mapping (most modern SaaS apps do), the access model is role-based (3-10 distinct roles), and you need more than yes/no app access governance but cannot justify a full connector.

This is the key innovation
Tier 2 gives you 80% of the governance value of a full connector at 5% of the cost and effort. The only thing you lose is the ability to see and manage app-native entitlements that do not map to IdP groups (e.g., Salesforce record-level sharing rules, ServiceNow ACLs). For most SaaS apps, role-level governance is sufficient.
Tier 3 App-Level IdP Groups -- Authentication Governance

What: One IdP group per app (e.g., App_Zoom_Users). SailPoint certifies whether the person should have access to the app at all. No visibility into in-app roles.

Apps: Low-risk SaaS apps where "can this person log in?" is the only governance question (Zoom, Webex, marketing tools, project management, etc.).

Source slots: 0 additional

Effort per app: 30 min config

Governance level: Authentication governance only. Yes/no access. No in-app role visibility.

When to use: The app has a flat access model (all users get the same permissions), the risk is low (no sensitive data, no admin functions worth governing), or the app does not support group-to-role mapping.

Tier 4 Discovery and Manual Governance -- The Unmanaged

What: Apps that are not federated to the IdP, not connected to SailPoint, and discovered through SSPM tools, browser extension telemetry, or expense report analysis. Governed through manual periodic reviews (spreadsheet-based attestation).

Apps: Shadow IT, free-tier SaaS, vendor portals, legacy web apps, apps authenticated via local credentials or social login.

Source slots: 0

Governance level: Discovery and inventory only. Manual certification outside SailPoint. Goal: identify and either federate (move to Tier 2/3) or decommission.

Tier 2 Deep-Dive: Role-Level IdP Group Governance

The technical pattern that makes authorization governance possible without custom connectors.

How It Works

  1. Design role-level groups in your IdP -- not one group per app, but one group per role per app. SF_Admin, SF_Standard, SF_ReadOnly instead of just Salesforce_Users.
  2. Configure the app to assign roles based on group membership -- most SaaS apps support this via SAML group claims, OIDC group scopes, or SCIM group push.
  3. SailPoint aggregates the groups as entitlements -- one Okta/Entra source surfaces all groups. Each group becomes a certifiable, requestable, provisionable entitlement in SailPoint.
  4. Create Access Profiles in SailPoint -- map each group to an Access Profile (e.g., "Salesforce Admin Access" = SF_Admin group).
  5. Create Access Applications -- each app gets a governance container in SailPoint linked to its Access Profiles.
  6. Certify, request, and automate -- managers certify "Does Jane need Salesforce Admin?" not just "Does Jane need Salesforce?" Lifecycle policies grant/revoke based on role changes.

Group Naming Convention

Consistency is critical when you have 200+ groups. Recommended pattern:

SP_{AppShortName}_{RoleName}

Examples:
SP_Salesforce_Admin
SP_Salesforce_Standard
SP_Salesforce_ReadOnly
SP_ServiceNow_ITIL

The SP_ prefix identifies SailPoint-managed groups and prevents collision with groups created for other purposes. SailPoint documentation recommends a SLPT_ prefix; use whatever is consistent in your org.

What the Manager Sees in Certification

Instead of a generic "Okta group: salesforce_users" (meaningless to most managers), they see:

The key
The display name and description in SailPoint come from the Access Profile, not from the raw group name. You control what the manager sees. Make it human-readable and decision-relevant: "Salesforce -- System Administrator" is certifiable. "SP_Salesforce_Admin" is not.

How the App Assigns Roles from Groups

The mechanism varies by app, but the pattern is the same: the IdP tells the app "this user is in group X" and the app maps group X to role Y.

MechanismHow It WorksApps That Support It
SAML Group ClaimsIdP sends a group list in the SAML assertion. App reads the claim and assigns roles based on group membership.Salesforce, ServiceNow, Snowflake, Jira Cloud, most enterprise SaaS
SCIM Group PushIdP pushes group membership to the app via SCIM 2.0 API. App maps SCIM groups to internal roles.Snowflake, ServiceNow, Salesforce, GitHub, Atlassian, Slack, Zoom
OIDC Group ScopeIdP includes groups in the OIDC token. App reads the token and assigns roles.Modern SaaS apps using OIDC, custom apps
IdP App-Level Role MappingThe IdP itself maps users to app roles (e.g., Okta app assignments with role attributes). No SAML claim needed -- the IdP provisions the role directly.Okta-integrated apps, Entra Enterprise Apps with App Roles
Important limitation
SAML group claims have limits: Okta returns max 100 groups per user. Entra SAML token limit is 150 groups, JWT is 200. Exceeding these limits causes complete omission of all groups, not truncation. Use group filtering in your IdP to only send relevant groups per app.

Configuration Walkthrough

Step-by-step: setting up Tier 2 role-level governance for a SaaS application using Okta as the IdP.

Step 1: Create Role-Level Groups in Okta

SP_Salesforce_Admin        -> Maps to Salesforce System Administrator profile
SP_Salesforce_Standard     -> Maps to Salesforce Standard User profile
SP_Salesforce_ReadOnly     -> Maps to Salesforce Read-Only profile

# Assign users to the appropriate group
# (This is what SailPoint will govern)

Step 2: Configure the App to Honor Group Claims

In the SaaS app's SSO/SAML settings, configure group-to-role mapping:

SP_Salesforce_StandardStandard User profileSame
SP_Salesforce_ReadOnlyRead-Only profileSame

Step 3: Configure SailPoint Access Profiles

In SailPoint ISC, create Access Profiles that wrap each Okta group entitlement:

Access Profile NameDescription (what managers see)SourceEntitlement
Salesforce -- System AdministratorFull admin access: user management, system configuration, data exportsOktaSP_Salesforce_Admin
Salesforce -- Standard UserStandard CRM access: contacts, leads, opportunities, reportsOktaSP_Salesforce_Standard
Salesforce -- Read OnlyView-only access to CRM data and reports. Cannot create or modify records.OktaSP_Salesforce_ReadOnly

Step 4: Create Access Application in SailPoint

Create a governance container for the app:

  1. Navigate to Applications > Access Applications > Create
  2. Name: "Salesforce"
  3. Source: your Okta source
  4. Link the three Access Profiles created above
  5. Configure governance: enable for certification campaigns, enable for access requests

Step 5: Repeat

Each app takes 2-4 hours of configuration. For 50 apps at 3 hours each, that is 150 hours of IAM team time -- roughly 4 weeks of dedicated work, or 2 months at 50% capacity. No engineering, no custom code, no professional services.

Honest Gaps: What This Model Cannot Do

No governance model is perfect. Here is where Tier 2 falls short and what to do about it.

GapWhat It MeansMitigation
No in-app fine-grained visibilityYou govern role-level access (Admin vs Standard) but cannot see or certify individual permissions within the role (e.g., Salesforce record-level sharing rules, specific API permissions).For apps where this matters (Salesforce, SAP), upgrade to Tier 1 with a full connector.
Group-to-role mapping driftIf someone manually changes a user's role inside the app (bypassing the IdP group), SailPoint does not detect it.Periodic manual reconciliation: compare IdP group membership to actual in-app roles. Some apps expose this via API for automated checking.
Not all apps support group-to-role mappingLegacy SaaS, small vendors, and some enterprise apps do not support SAML group claims or SCIM group push.These apps fall to Tier 3 (app-level yes/no governance) or require a flat file/custom connector.
Group proliferation100 apps x 4 roles average = 400 IdP groups. This is manageable but requires disciplined naming conventions and periodic cleanup.Enforce the naming convention. Use SailPoint Roles to bundle related groups. Automate group lifecycle.
No provisioning to the appTier 2 provisions to the IdP (adds/removes group membership). The app must react to the group change. If the app does not support JIT or SCIM, the user may need to log out and back in for changes to take effect.Use SCIM where available (near-real-time). For SAML-only, changes take effect on next login.
Revocation gapRemoving a user from an IdP group removes their SSO access (they cannot log in). But some apps keep a local account active until explicitly deprovisioned.For sensitive apps, pair Tier 2 with periodic app-level reconciliation or use SCIM for real deprovisioning.
Auditor acceptance variesSome auditors accept IdP group-level certification as sufficient for compliance. Others want to see in-app entitlement-level evidence.Discuss with your auditors early. Frame Tier 2 as "role-based access certification" with evidence of the group-to-role mapping configuration as supplementary documentation.
The 80/20 principle applies to gaps too
Most of these gaps only matter for your top 5-10 critical applications -- which are already on Tier 1 with full connectors. For the other 90+ apps, role-level governance is sufficient, and auditors will accept it when you can demonstrate the group-to-role mapping is configured and enforced.

Governing the Long Tail

What to do with the 50-100 apps that do not neatly fit into Tiers 1-3.

Pattern A: Federate First, Govern Second

The fastest way to bring an ungoverned app into the governance framework is to federate it to your IdP. Once it is behind Okta/Entra SSO, you can immediately apply Tier 2 or Tier 3 governance. The priority order:

  1. Does the app support SAML/OIDC? -> Federate it -> Tier 2 or 3
  2. Does the app support SCIM? -> Enable SCIM provisioning -> Tier 2 with group push
  3. Neither? -> Flat file export (one source slot) or manual governance (Tier 4)

Pattern B: SSPM as the Discovery Layer

Tools like Nudge Security, Grip Security, and Valence discover SaaS applications across your organization -- including shadow IT, OAuth grants, and apps authenticated via personal credentials.

The SSPM tool becomes your Tier 4 layer:

  • Discover: Find every SaaS app in use (not just IT-managed ones)
  • Classify: Risk-rank each app based on data sensitivity, user count, and security posture
  • Promote: High-risk apps get federated and moved to Tier 2; low-risk apps stay at Tier 3/4
  • Monitor: Continuously watch for new SaaS adoption and OAuth sprawl

Pattern C: Composite Sources for Flat File Apps

If you must govern apps via flat file but are hitting source limits, consider a composite source pattern: aggregate multiple small apps into a single flat file source with an application identifier column.

Instead of 20 flat file sources (20 source slots), create 1 source with a applicationName column that identifies which app each account belongs to. Use SailPoint Access Profiles to separate the apps logically. This is not officially supported as a best practice, but some organizations use it to stay within source limits.

Trade-off
Composite sources make the data model more complex and can cause issues with entitlement cross-referencing. Use this pattern only when source limits are a hard constraint and you cannot upgrade your ISC tier.

Pattern D: Governance Beyond SailPoint

For some organizations, the honest answer is: SailPoint governs the core, and a parallel process governs the long tail.

  • SailPoint handles Tier 1 and Tier 2 apps (automated, continuous, audit-grade)
  • A quarterly manual review process handles Tier 3 and 4 apps:
    • App owner exports user list
    • IAM team facilitates manager review (spreadsheet or lightweight tool)
    • Evidence archived in GRC tool or shared drive
  • This is how most 1,000-5,000 identity organizations actually operate
  • Auditors accept this when you can demonstrate: (a) risk-based tiering, (b) consistent review cadence, (c) evidence retention

Implementation Roadmap

A phased approach to implementing the 4-tier model over 6 months.

Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1-2)

Connect Tier 1 sources + establish IdP governance foundation
  • Deploy SailPoint ISC with core Tier 1 connectors (AD, Entra, Workday/SAP HR, Okta)
  • Establish identity correlation and lifecycle states (JML)
  • Design the group naming convention (SP_{App}_{Role})
  • Document the tier classification criteria

Phase 2: First Wave of Tier 2 (Month 3)

10 highest-risk SaaS apps via role-level IdP groups
  • Classify your SaaS portfolio: risk-rank all apps by data sensitivity, user count, and admin capability
  • Select top 10 apps for Tier 2 (Salesforce, Jira, Snowflake, AWS, etc.)
  • Create role-level groups in Okta/Entra for each app (3-5 groups per app = 30-50 groups)
  • Configure group-to-role mapping in each app (SAML claims or SCIM push)
  • Create Access Profiles and Access Applications in SailPoint
  • Run a test certification campaign to validate

Phase 3: Scale Tier 2 + Add Tier 3 (Months 4-5)

Expand to 50+ Tier 2 apps and 50+ Tier 3 apps
  • Batch the remaining SaaS apps: 10-15 per week
  • Apps that support group-to-role mapping -> Tier 2
  • Apps with flat access models or no role mapping -> Tier 3
  • Remaining Tier 1 connectors (Salesforce full connector, ServiceNow, CyberArk)
  • Begin regular certification campaigns across all tiers

Phase 4: Long Tail + Discovery (Month 6+)

Tier 4 discovery, manual governance for stragglers, optimization
  • Deploy SSPM tool (or analyze IdP/CASB logs) to discover ungoverned SaaS
  • Classify discovered apps: federate to IdP (promote to Tier 2/3) or accept risk with manual review
  • Establish quarterly manual review cadence for Tier 4 apps
  • Optimize: review group-to-role mappings, clean up unused groups, refine certification campaigns
  • Document the architecture for auditors: tier rationale, evidence of governance at each tier
